Research

2019 — 2024 PhD Thesis

Comparative anatomy of amygdala–frontal circuits

  • First use of MAPseq — an RNA-barcode neuroanatomy technique — in macaques to characterize single-neuron projection patterns from basolateral amygdala to frontal cortex (Neuron 2023).
  • Extended to mice, revealing significant species differences in the multi-area wiring of branching amygdala neurons (Current Biology 2024).
  • Identified a substantial proportion of branching neurons innervating multiple distinct cortical areas — a candidate anatomical substrate for brain-wide broadcasting.

MAPseq · Single-neuron tracing · Comparative anatomy · Amygdala → frontal

2019 — 2024 Cross-species

Hierarchy of intrinsic timescales across species

  • Analyzed ~20,000 single neurons across mice, macaques, and humans.
  • Identified consistent hierarchies of intrinsic timescales in frontal and limbic regions — a conserved organizing principle of cortical circuits.
  • Timescales index how long a neuron's activity integrates information over time, bridging single-cell properties and network-level computation.

Intrinsic timescales · Cross-species · Population dynamics · Frontal & limbic

2024 — present Postdoctoral

Circuit physiology of reward learning in macaques

  • Trained 5 rhesus macaques on a probabilistic reversal-learning task with variable trace intervals (up to 3 s), recording from amygdala and frontal cortex across 200,000+ trials.
  • Identified a specific credit-assignment deficit when choice and outcome are separated in time, with strongest neural effects in amygdala, agranular insula, and ventromedial PFC.
  • Complemented by data-constrained recurrent neural network models and ongoing chemogenetic inactivation of amygdala → vlPFC projections.
  • Current analyses target inter-region communication channels underlying credit assignment.

Reversal learning · Credit assignment · Amygdala → vlPFC · Chemogenetics
